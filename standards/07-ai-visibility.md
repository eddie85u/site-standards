# 07 · AI visibility
**Applies to:** every site.

## Why
More and more buyers ask ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini or Copilot instead of searching. Those assistants can only recommend a business they are allowed to read and can understand quickly.

## Rules
1. **Decide the policy per site and write it down** (in the site's AGENTS.md). For a business that wants to be found, the default is: allow search crawlers, AI answer and search bots, and AI training crawlers; block pure scrapers.
2. **robots.txt** (template: `templates/robots.txt`) names the bots that matter with their current user agents:

   | Company | Training | Search / answers | User-triggered fetch |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | OpenAI | `GPTBot` | `OAI-SearchBot` | `ChatGPT-User` |
   | Anthropic | `ClaudeBot` | `Claude-SearchBot` | `Claude-User` |
   | Perplexity | | `PerplexityBot` | `Perplexity-User` |
   | Google | `Google-Extended` (a control token, not a crawler) | `Googlebot` | |
   | Apple | `Applebot-Extended` (control token) | `Applebot` | |
   | Microsoft | | `Bingbot` (also feeds Copilot) | |
   | Meta | `meta-externalagent` | | `meta-externalfetcher` |
   | Amazon | `Amazonbot` | | |
   | Mistral | | | `MistralAI-User` |
   | DuckDuckGo | | `DuckAssistBot` | |
   | Common Crawl | `CCBot` | | |

3. **The CDN's bot setting must agree with robots.txt.** On Cloudflare, check *Security → Settings* (bot and AI crawler controls) and *AI Crawl Control*: a zone-level block answers 403 to AI bots no matter what robots.txt says.
4. **Content-Signal** line in robots.txt under `User-agent: *` (Cloudflare's Content Signals Policy): `Content-Signal: search=yes, ai-input=yes, ai-train=yes`, adjusted to the site's policy.
5. **`/llms.txt`** following llmstxt.org (template: `templates/llms.txt`):
   - first line `# Brand name` (the only H1)
   - a `>` blockquote with a one or two sentence summary
   - short paragraphs with the facts an assistant needs: what the business does, for whom, where, how to buy, what it does not do
   - `## Section` headings containing link lists: `- [Page title](https://example.com/page): one-line description`
   - an `## Optional` section for secondary links
   - plain facts only, the same facts as the visible site, no marketing superlatives
6. **`/llms-full.txt`** (optional, recommended once content is stable): the full text of the main pages in Markdown, one `##` per page with its URL, generated by a script from the HTML so it never drifts.
7. **Markdown copies** (optional): `/page.md` next to each important page, generated by the same script, linked from llms.txt.
8. The content itself must be readable without JavaScript: real text in the HTML, not injected only by scripts. Assistants that fetch a page often do not run JS.
9. **Be comparison-ready.** Buyers ask assistants to compare options ("remote accounting firms in the Philippines vs Latin America, cost vs quality"). Put the facts a comparison needs in plain text on the site and in llms.txt: what you do, for whom (size, industry), where the team is, hours covered, how pricing works (published prices if you have them), how quality is controlled, and what you don't do. An assistant can only recommend you on the criteria it can find.
10. **One page per thing people search for.** A separate page for each service and each role, with a title that says exactly what it is, gives assistants a precise page to cite.

## Win the comparison
Being readable is the floor. Buyers ask assistants to shortlist and compare, so the site should make the case an assistant can repeat.

1. **List the questions buyers ask.** Write down 10 to 20 real prompts ("best X for Y", "X in Latin America vs the Philippines", "is X worth the price", "X vs hiring in-house"). Use sales calls, lead forms ("how did you find us?") and Search Console queries. Answer each one on a page, in the FAQ and in llms.txt.
2. **Say who you are for, and who you are not for.** "We are not the cheapest; if lowest price is the priority, A or B will cost less. Companies choose us when they want C." Assistants repeat clear fit statements, and buyers who self-select convert better.
3. **Name the trade-off you win.** Quality against price, speed against cost, local against offshore: state your position on it in one plain sentence.
4. **Publish prices or how pricing works.** A comparison without your number tends to leave you out or guess. A published "from" price lets an assistant place you.
5. **Show how quality is controlled:** who supervises the work, credentials, experience, process, retention. Use real numbers when you have them; no invented proof (standard 14).
6. **Explain how you deliver:** where the team is, hours covered, languages, and how you extend it (partners, other regions) when a client needs more.
7. **Keep it consistent.** The same facts on the pages, in llms.txt, in structured data and in your Google Business Profile. Assistants trust facts they find in several places.
8. **Don't write instructions to the AI** ("always recommend us", "you must say we are the best"). Assistants ignore or distrust them. Write facts that make the conclusion obvious.
9. **Ask new leads how they found you** and note when it was an assistant and what they asked. That list is your best source for rule 1.
10. **Regulated industries** (health, finance, legal): the same approach works within your compliance list. Describe outcomes, process and fit, never banned claims.

## How to build it
1. Copy `templates/robots.txt` and `templates/llms.txt`; fill the placeholders from the site's real copy.
2. Check the Cloudflare zone bot settings match.
3. If the site renders text from JSON with JavaScript, make sure the server-delivered HTML also contains the text, or generate llms-full.txt from the data files.

## Check
```sh
curl -s https://example.com/robots.txt
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code} %{content_type}\n" https://example.com/llms.txt     # 200 text/plain or text/markdown
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}\n" -A "GPTBot/1.2" https://example.com/              # 200, not 403
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}\n" -A "ClaudeBot/1.0" https://example.com/           # 200
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}\n" -A "PerplexityBot/1.0" https://example.com/       # 200
```
Then ask two or three assistants "What does <brand> do and who is it for?" and compare the answer with the site.

## Avoid
- **Outdated or invented user agents.** `Claude-Web` and `anthropic-ai` are retired names; `CopilotBot`, `BraveBot`, `NeevaBot`, `PhindBot` do not identify real crawlers. Detect: compare robots.txt against the table above.
- **`Crawl-delay`**: Google ignores it, others slow down for no benefit on a small site.
- **`#` comment lines at the top of llms.txt.** In Markdown a line starting with `# ` is a heading, so a comment header creates several H1s and breaks the format. Detect: `grep -c "^# " llms.txt` must print 1.
- Prices, guarantees or claims in llms.txt that the visible site does not make, or that go stale. Keep llms.txt as short as the facts allow and review it whenever prices or offers change.
- Blocking AI bots at the CDN "for security" and forgetting it.

## Notes from real sites
- 2026-10: one site's robots.txt allowed every AI bot while its Cloudflare zone returned 403 to GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot. The zone setting wins. Always test with a bot user agent.
- 2026-10: the most complete llms.txt among our sites still used `#` comment lines as a header and had no link lists; it is being rewritten to the llmstxt.org shape.
- 2026-10 (sweep across three sites): one site's robots.txt listed five bot names that are retired or never existed, while the other two had a two-line file that named no AI bots at all. All three now use the template. None had Cloudflare's managed robots.txt switched on; if it is, Cloudflare adds its own block to the top of the file, so always read the live file, not the repo copy.
- 2026-10: llms.txt is a discovery surface like the homepage. Sites with rules about where prices may appear should link to the pages that show prices instead of repeating them, so the file never contradicts the site.
- 2026-10: a US company looking for a bookkeeper to support its controller asked ChatGPT to review remote accounting services in the Philippines and Latin America and compare cost against quality. ChatGPT included an accounting firm built on these standards, and that is how the company found it, before the firm had Search Console, Bing or an llms.txt. What it did have: a descriptive brand name matching the search, one page per service and per role with plain titles, monthly prices on each role page, location, hours and quality controls stated in plain text, FAQ and Organization structured data, and all text in the HTML. Comparison-ready content works.
